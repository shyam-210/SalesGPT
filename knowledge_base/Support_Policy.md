## Severity Level Definitions

### Severity 1: Critical

**Definition:**
- Production system completely down
- Data loss or corruption
- Security breach
- Service unavailable to end users

**Examples:**
- All instances in production environment unreachable
- Database corruption causing data loss
- DDoS attack taking down website

**Response:**
- Immediate escalation to senior engineers
- Continuous updates every 30 minutes until resolved
- Post-mortem report provided within 5 business days

### Severity 2: High

**Definition:**
- Major functionality impaired
- Significant performance degradation
- Workaround available but not ideal

**Examples:**
- Autoscaling not working (manual scaling possible)
- 50% of instances experiencing high latency
- API rate limits incorrectly applied

**Response:**
- Assigned to experienced engineer
- Updates every 2 hours until resolved

### Severity 3: Medium

**Definition:**
- Minor functionality issue
- General "how-to" questions
- Feature requests

**Examples:**
- Console UI bug (doesn't block work)
- Question about best practices
- Request for documentation clarification

**Response:**
- Assigned to support engineer
- Resolution within SLA timeframe

### Severity 4: Low

**Definition:**
- Cosmetic issues
- General inquiries
- Non-urgent feature requests

**Examples:**
- Typo in documentation
- Question about future roadmap
- Request for new region availability

**Response:**
- Handled as time permits
- May be redirected to community forum or documentation

## Support Best Practices

### How to Get Faster Support

1. **Choose the Right Channel:**
   - Critical issues → Phone (Enterprise) or Email with "Severity 1" subject
   - Quick questions → Chat
   - General inquiries → Community forum

2. **Provide Detailed Information:**
   - Account ID
   - Affected resource IDs (instance IDs, bucket names, etc.)
   - Error messages (full text, not screenshots of partial messages)
   - Steps to reproduce
   - What you've already tried

3. **Set Correct Severity:**
   - Don't mark everything as Severity 1
   - Use severity definitions above

4. **Follow Up Appropriately:**
   - Check spam folder for responses
   - Respond to support engineer questions promptly
   - Don't open duplicate tickets (slows down response)

### Escalation Process

If you're not satisfied with support:

**Step 1:** Reply to your ticket asking for escalation

**Step 2:** Email: support-escalation@teamdefaulters.com

**Step 3 (Enterprise only):** Contact your TAM directly

**Step 4 (Enterprise only):** Request escalation to VP of Customer Success

**Response Time for Escalations:**
- Professional: 4 hours
- Enterprise: 1 hour
